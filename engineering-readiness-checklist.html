Before your next hardware build: an engineering readiness checklist

Ten questions to turn “we need to move forward” into a clearer engineering decision.

By Faheem Faheem, Ph.D. · October 24, 2026 · GeminiWave

For engineering leaders and hardware founders in New England and beyond. Use this checklist with your team before a prototype build, qualification review, manufacturing transfer, or new R&D effort.

Open access · No signup required

How to use it: Check a question when your team can point to an answer and supporting evidence. For each open item, record the decision, missing information, owner, and next action. Checks remain only while this page is open.

01 / DIAGNOSE

Simulation & reliability

  1. Record the operating conditions, affected parts, observed behavior, and what changed between working and failing units.

  2. Identify material properties, interfaces, thermal paths, loads, boundary conditions, and the uncertainty that could change the decision.

  3. Choose the inspection, measurement, or focused test that would help separate likely causes.

02 / DEVELOP

Product & wearable feasibility

  1. Define the intended use, user needs, and measurable criteria. For wearable concepts, include heat, size, interfaces, power, and usability.

  2. Separate hardware, sensing, software, and specialist contributions. Identify the smallest useful prototype and its limitations.

03 / PREPARE

Verification & manufacturing

  1. List the test method, acceptance criteria, responsible person, and current result or evidence gap.

  2. Review drawings, materials, assembly instructions, supplier questions, inspection points, and unresolved changes.

  3. Make engineering, quality, supplier, laboratory, and manufacturing responsibilities explicit, including who makes the release decision.

04 / PLAN

Roadmaps & R&D proposals

  1. Compare the customer problem, technical feasibility, available resources, and evidence needed before committing to another phase.

  2. Check that work packages, milestones, partner roles, budget assumptions, and transition goals form a coherent technical case.

What to bring to a fit call

Start with one obstacle and the decision your team needs to make.

  • A high-level product description and target milestone.
  • Your main uncertainty and what you have already tried.
  • Known timing, team, supplier, and testing constraints.
  • The kind of help you need: assessment, defined project, or ongoing guidance.

Keep the initial conversation nonconfidential. Share only information you are authorized to disclose. Scope, conflict checks, confidentiality arrangements and a suitable channel come before technical-file review.
